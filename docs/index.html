The control

Free throws should have shown nothing. They showed −3.

Nobody guards a free throw: same rim, same distance, no defender. If the method were measuring league difficulty, the free-throw coefficient would come out near zero. It did not. It came out at -3.3 for the NCAA and -3.2 for the WNBA — a drop of about three points in both.

That cannot be league difficulty. It is a flat cost of playing FIBA at all: travel, unfamiliar teammates, a different ball, compressed schedules. And because it is common to both leagues, it sits on every absolute gap alike.

So absolute club→FIBA gaps are not interpretable as league strength. The difference between two leagues is, because the shared offset cancels. On that scale the control passes, at -0.1 points.

Estimated FIBA environment offset: -2.4 points. Worth its own scepticism — the control's interval spans 9 points, so it could not have detected an offset difference the size of the three-point effect. It bounds the method loosely, not tightly.

Is the environment offset really one number?

The method assumes a single FIBA environment offset. Estimated separately per competition, the control does not look like one number at all.

Free-throw gap by event, against a pooled offset of -2.4. The spread is about nine points, so the offset looks like a property of the event rather than of FIBA. This weakens the adjusted per-league columns and leaves the league differences untouched, since a difference cancels whatever the offset is — a stronger reason to lead with them than the control alone provided. No interval is drawn: these are single pooled gaps, not fitted estimates.

Known defects, found and handled

  • fixedThe club leaderboard silently capped responses at the page limit, returning exactly 4,096 NCAA records per season. Paging to exhaustion returns over 20,000. Coefficients moved by less than half a point, which is a useful robustness check.
  • droppedField goals. The club endpoint's fgMade/fgAttempt are not field goals: A'ja Wilson's 2025 season reads 950/1940 against a true 2PM+3PM of 459/931. The points identity closes on the explicit shot fields and not at all on the fg ones. The FIBA endpoint is unaffected.
  • fixedInverse-variance weights were computed from each player's own observed rate, so an 8-for-8 line gave a variance of zero and an exploding weight. Weights now depend on sample size alone.

Limitations

  • Cell sizes are 39–89 players. The three-point difference does not clear zero and is not a result.
  • Players are matched by name, not id. See the audit table; the pairs are not individually hand-verified.
  • Selection. Players who reach a national team are not a random sample of their league, and those who play enough FIBA minutes to qualify are a further cut.
  • The environment offset rests on one control statistic, with a wide interval. League differences do not depend on it; the adjusted per-league figures do.
  • Shooting only. Assists, rebounds and steals are not available from the endpoint used.
  • Season pairing is coarse — club seasons are matched to tournaments by year rather than by sequence.
  • Intervals are approximate. The shrunk estimate's variance treats its two components as independent and ignores uncertainty in the between-league variance.
